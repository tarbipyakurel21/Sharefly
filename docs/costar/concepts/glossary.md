# Commercial Real Estate Glossary

Short definitions of the commercial real estate (CRE) terms that come up most often when working with the property, listing, tenant and sale data in this kit. Residential terms are on [residential-listings-and-sales.md](residential-listings-and-sales.md).

## Property and building

- **Property**: the real estate asset, usually one building or a group of buildings on a parcel. Listings, tenants and sales all attach to a property.
- **Building class (A, B, C)**: a subjective, market-relative quality grade. Class A is the most prestigious and commands above-average rents, Class B is the middle of the range, Class C is older and basic with below-average rents. A Class A building in one market is not the same quality as a Class A building in another.
- **CoStar Building Rating (1 to 5 stars)**: a quality rating applied on one national scale and judged against peers of the same property type, so it can be compared across markets. 5 is best in class (typically new, well amenitized); 1 is well below average. Older material calls this the "Star Rating"; it is the same attribute.
- **GBA (gross building area)**: the whole building, measured to the outside of the exterior walls, including elevator shafts, stairs and common areas.
- **RBA (rentable building area)**: the portion of the building that is rentable. It is GBA less vertical penetrations (elevator shafts, stairwells) and still includes the tenants' share of common areas. It is the denominator for most leasing statistics.
- **GLA (gross leasable area)**: the term used for retail space; for practical purposes it is the retail equivalent of RBA.
- **Usable area**: space a tenant occupies exclusively, excluding shared areas.
- **Common area**: shared space such as lobbies, corridors and restrooms.
- **Load factor (add-on factor)**: the ratio used to convert usable area to rentable area. A 15% load factor means usable area multiplied by 1.15 gives rentable area. Some sources define the opposite quantity ("loss factor", the share of the rentable area that is common), so check which basis a figure uses.
- **Efficiency ratio**: rentable area divided by gross area. For example 11,500 SF rentable in 13,000 SF gross is 0.885.
- **Typical floor**: the most common floor size in a building.
- **Floor plate**: the area of a single floor.
- **Parking ratio**: parking spaces per 1,000 SF of rentable (or leasable) area.
- **Clear height**: the vertical distance from floor to the lowest overhead obstruction, such as a beam, sprinkler or duct. It matters most for warehouses.
- **Loading dock and drive-in door**: a dock is a platform at truck-bed height; a drive-in door lets a truck drive into the building at ground level.
- **Floor area ratio (FAR)**: building floor area divided by land area. See [metrics and formulas](metrics-and-formulas.md#2-size-and-density).
- **Adaptive reuse**: converting a building to a different use, such as a factory to offices.
- **Anchor tenant**: a credit-worthy tenant that draws traffic to a retail center, such as a supermarket in a neighborhood center.
- **CBD**: central business district, the downtown core.

## Apartments

See [apartments.md](apartments.md) for how these appear in the data.

- **Apartment community**: a property rented out unit by unit, in one building or several. Buildings of five or more units are treated as commercial real estate.
- **Floor plan**: one unit layout offered in a community, with its bedrooms, bathrooms and size.
- **Unit mix**: the share of a community's units by number of bedrooms.
- **Garden apartments**: low buildings set among landscaped grounds and surface parking, usually walk-ups.
- **Low-rise, mid-rise, high-rise**: buildings of about one to three stories, about four to nine, and ten or more.
- **Walk-up**: a building with no elevator.
- **Market-rate**: rents set by the market.
- **Affordable**: rents held below market for tenants who qualify by income, under a program such as the Low-Income Housing Tax Credit or a government rent subsidy.
- **Rent control and rent stabilization**: local laws that limit rents or how fast they rise.
- **Individually metered and master metered**: whether each unit has its own utility meters, or the building has one and the owner pays.
- **Concession**: a discount offered to sign a lease, such as a month of free rent.
- **Price per unit**: sale price divided by the number of units, the standard price measure for apartments.

## Space and availability

- **Space**: a defined area within a building that can be occupied or marketed (a suite, a floor, a unit).
- **Direct space**: space offered for lease by the landlord or owner.
- **Relet space**: space that was previously occupied and is being leased again by the owner. In most reporting "direct" space means relet plus new space.
- **New (shell) space**: space that has never been occupied or built out.
- **Sublet (sublease) space**: space already leased by a tenant that the tenant offers to others.
- **Assignment**: a tenant transfers its entire lease to another party.
- **Coworking**: flexible shared office space let by the desk or the office, usually on short terms.
- **Available space**: space marketed for lease or sale. It can be vacant, still occupied, sublet, or available at a future date.
- **Vacant space**: space not physically occupied, whether or not a lease obligation exists on it.
- **Vacant available space**: space that is both empty and marketed.
- **Leased but vacant**: a lease exists but the tenant has not moved in.
- **Occupied space**: space that is physically occupied by a tenant.
- **Contiguous space**: space that is or can be combined into one block. "Contiguous on floor" means touching spaces on one floor. "Contiguous in building" means blocks on vertically adjoining floors, such as two full floors or a full floor plus part of the next.
- **Existing inventory**: buildings with a certificate of occupancy that can be occupied. It excludes planned and under-construction space.

## Rent and lease terms

- **Asking rent (face rent)**: the rate the landlord is asking. In Houston it is quoted per square foot per year; in Los Angeles it is usually quoted per square foot per month.
- **Contract rent**: the rent written into the lease.
- **Effective rent**: contract rent adjusted for concessions (free rent, allowances) and, depending on the method, escalations and tenant-paid costs. See [metrics and formulas](metrics-and-formulas.md#effective-rent-and-net-effective-rent).
- **Market rent**: the rent a property would most probably command in the open market.
- **NNN (triple net)**: the tenant pays rent plus taxes, insurance and maintenance. Single net and double net cover fewer of these.
- **Full service gross (FSG)**: the landlord pays the normal building operating costs out of a single quoted rent.
- **Modified gross**: a middle arrangement where some expenses (for example electricity or cleaning) are paid separately.
- **Industrial gross**: a modified gross form common in industrial buildings; the exact split is set in each lease.
- **CAM (common area maintenance)**: charges passed to tenants for cleaning and maintaining shared areas.
- **Base year / expense stop**: a clause under which the landlord covers operating costs up to a set level and the tenant pays increases above it.
- **Escalation**: a contractual rent increase, either a fixed percentage, a fixed dollar amount, or tied to an index such as CPI. NNN retail leases often step up every five years rather than every year.
- **Concessions**: inducements to sign, mainly free rent, tenant improvement allowances, moving allowances and lease buyouts.
- **TI (tenant improvement) allowance**: money the landlord contributes to build out the space, usually quoted per square foot.
- **Free rent**: months at the start (or end) of the lease with no rent due.
- **Lease commencement date**: when the lease term begins. **Rent commencement date**: when rent payments begin. If rent starts later, the gap is a free-rent period.
- **Renewal**: a new lease term signed by a tenant already in the space.
- **Lease comp**: a completed lease transaction used as a comparable.
- **Rollover / lease expiration risk**: the share of income tied to leases ending in a given period.
- **WALT**: weighted average lease term, usually weighted by rent or area.

## Sales and investment

- **Sale comp**: a completed sale used as a comparable for valuing other properties.
- **Investment sale**: a buyer purchases to lease the property out for income. **Owner/user sale**: a buyer purchases to occupy the property for its own business.
- **Arm's length sale**: a sale between unrelated parties acting in their own interest. Non-arm's-length sales (between related parties) are usually excluded from valuation work.
- **Distress sale**: a sale under pressure outside the owner's control, such as foreclosure or bankruptcy, usually below market value.
- **REO (real estate owned)**: property taken back by a lender through foreclosure and then sold.
- **Portfolio sale**: several properties sold in one transaction. If the price for each property is unknown, an allocated price may be estimated.
- **Sale-leaseback**: an owner sells the property and leases it back from the buyer.
- **1031 exchange**: a US tax rule that lets a seller defer capital gains by reinvesting in similar property.
- **Fee simple**: full ownership of real property. **Leasehold**: the right to use property under a lease. **Ground lease**: a long lease of land (often 20 to 99 years) on which the tenant builds or operates.
- **Recorded owner**: the name on the deed, often a single-purpose company. **True owner**: the real entity behind the recorded owner.
- **Non-disclosure state**: a state, such as Texas, where sale prices are not part of the public record.
- **NOI (net operating income)**: income from the property less operating expenses, before debt service, capital items and income taxes.
- **Cap rate (capitalization rate)**: NOI divided by property value or price.
- **GIM / GRM**: gross income multiplier and gross rent multiplier, price divided by gross income or gross rent.

## Market statistics

These are measures over a set of buildings. The kit has the building-level inputs, not the published market figures.

- **Inventory**: total existing building area in a market.
- **Vacancy rate**: vacant area divided by existing rentable area.
- **Availability rate**: available area divided by existing rentable area.
- **Percent leased**: the share of a building's rentable area under lease, whether or not the tenants have moved in.
- **Absorption**: change in occupied space over a period. Gross absorption counts space moved into; net absorption subtracts space vacated.
- **Leasing activity**: total area of lease transactions in a period.
- **Deliveries**: new buildings completed in a period. **Under construction**: buildings being built, counted from when the foundation is in place.
- **Market and submarket**: competitive areas defined by property type. They do not follow city or county lines. See [geography and markets](geography-and-markets.md).
- **CBSA**: core-based statistical area, a US Census Bureau grouping of a core city and surrounding counties (metropolitan or micropolitan).

## Hotels

- **Rooms**: the number of guest rooms, the hotel's measure of size.
- **ADR (average daily rate)**: room revenue divided by rooms sold, the average price of an occupied room for one night.
- **Chain scale (class)**: a hotel's price tier, from Economy through Midscale, Upper Midscale, Upscale and Upper Upscale to Luxury.
- **Franchise and independent**: a franchised hotel operates under a brand it licenses; an independent hotel has no brand.
